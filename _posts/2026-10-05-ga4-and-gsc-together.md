GA4 and Google Search Console are both important for SEO, but they do not measure the same thing.

I use Search Console to understand performance in Google Search and GA4 to understand behavior after a visitor reaches the website.

Keeping those roles separate makes reporting clearer.

## What Google Search Console tells me

Search Console helps answer questions such as:

- Which queries show the website?
- How many impressions does it receive?
- How many clicks come from Google Search?
- What is the click-through rate?
- What is the average position?
- Which pages receive search clicks?

This is the visibility layer of my SEO reporting.

## What GA4 tells me

GA4 helps answer different questions:

- How many organic sessions occurred?
- Which landing pages did users enter?
- Were sessions engaged?
- Which pages did users view?
- Which tracked events occurred?
- Did visitors click calls, directions, WhatsApp or booking buttons?

This is the on-site behavior layer.

## Why the numbers do not match

It is common to see different figures between Search Console and GA4.

That does not automatically mean the tracking is broken.

The platforms use different definitions, processing methods and attribution logic. Search Console is focused on Google Search activity, while GA4 measures website sessions and events.

I therefore avoid putting a Search Console click number beside a GA4 session number and treating the difference as an error without investigation.

## Example from a restaurant project

During a measured period, Google Search Console recorded 55.3K impressions and 2.12K clicks.

GA4 recorded 2,804 organic sessions, including 2,050 engaged sessions and a 73.1% engagement rate.

The value is not in forcing these figures to match.

The value is in using them to answer two connected questions:

**Did the website earn search visibility and clicks?**

**What happened after organic visitors reached the site?**

## Example from a salon project

For a salon project, Search Console recorded 492 clicks and 4.32K impressions.

GA4 recorded 668 organic sessions and 482 engaged organic sessions, along with tracked actions such as price-list views, service clicks and WhatsApp clicks.

Together, those datasets create a clearer picture of performance.

## My reporting structure

I prefer separating an SEO report into:

1. Search visibility — Search Console
2. Organic website behavior — GA4
3. High-intent events — GA4/GTM
4. Local profile actions — Google Business Profile, when relevant

This makes the source of each metric clear.

## Better reporting starts with better definitions

Before presenting a KPI, I ask what it actually represents.

A click is not necessarily a session. A WhatsApp click is not necessarily a lead. A booking click is not necessarily a completed booking.

Clear definitions make marketing reports more credible and more useful for decision-making.
