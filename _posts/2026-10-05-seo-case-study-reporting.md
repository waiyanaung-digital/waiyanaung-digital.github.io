An SEO case study should demonstrate results without pretending that every improvement came from one action.

This became important to me while building my own portfolio. Real marketing projects involve multiple channels, changing demand, brand activity and platform behavior.

A credible case study needs context.

## 1. State the measurement source

I label where each metric comes from.

For example:

- Google Search Console: clicks, impressions, CTR and average position
- GA4: sessions, engagement, page views and events
- Google Business Profile: profile views, calls, directions and website clicks
- Meta Ads Manager: paid campaign delivery and cost metrics

This prevents unrelated metrics from being blended into one performance claim.

## 2. Define the time period

“Traffic increased” is incomplete without knowing when.

I include the period being compared whenever possible.

For one restaurant project, monthly Search Console impressions increased 679% and monthly clicks increased 507% from December 2025 to August 2026.

That is more precise than simply saying “SEO grew by 679%.”

## 3. Separate correlation from causation

Marketing performance rarely has one cause.

SEO results can be influenced by:

- Technical improvements
- Content changes
- Local optimization
- Brand awareness
- Seasonality
- Search demand
- Offline activity
- Other marketing channels
- Google's ranking systems

I can explain what I worked on and what changed during the project, but I should not claim that one optimization caused every result unless the evidence supports that conclusion.

## 4. Report actions accurately

Event names matter.

If GA4 records a `whatsapp_click`, I report a WhatsApp click.

I do not automatically call it a qualified lead.

If a visitor clicks a booking button, I report a booking action unless I have data confirming the completed appointment.

This may sound conservative, but it makes the case study more trustworthy.

## 5. Show both visibility and behavior

A strong SEO case study should go beyond rankings.

I like to include:

### Search visibility

Impressions, clicks, CTR and relevant query positions.

### Website behavior

Organic sessions, engaged sessions and landing-page performance.

### Intent signals

Calls, directions, menu clicks, WhatsApp clicks, booking actions or other relevant events.

The exact metrics depend on the business.

## 6. Explain what you actually did

Metrics without strategy do not show skill.

A case study should explain the work behind the project, such as:

- Keyword and search-intent analysis
- On-page optimization
- Local SEO improvements
- Google Business Profile management
- Website structure
- GA4/GTM event tracking
- Search Console monitoring
- Reporting and iteration

This helps the reader understand the process rather than only seeing a dashboard screenshot.

## 7. Include limitations

Limitations are not weaknesses.

They demonstrate that the person presenting the case study understands measurement.

For example, if revenue attribution is unavailable, I can focus on search visibility, engagement and tracked customer actions instead of inventing an ROI figure.

## A simple SEO case-study structure

I use this structure:

1. Business context
2. Objective
3. Work completed
4. Measurement setup
5. Search performance
6. Website behavior
7. High-intent actions
8. Key lessons
9. Measurement limitations

This creates a story that is useful to both marketers and potential employers or clients.
