Google Business Profile is one of the most important touchpoints for a local business. A potential customer can discover the business in Google Search or Maps, view photos, check its location, call, request directions or visit the website — sometimes without visiting the website first.

While working on the digital presence of a Dubai restaurant, I learned that Google Business Profile should not be managed as an isolated listing. I treat it as part of the wider local SEO system.

## 1. Start with accurate business information

The foundation is simple: the information customers see should be correct and consistent.

I review the core profile information, including:

- Business name
- Primary and relevant secondary categories
- Address and map location
- Opening hours
- Phone number
- Website
- Business description
- Relevant services or attributes

Incorrect information creates friction at exactly the point when someone may be ready to visit or contact the business.

## 2. Choose categories based on the actual business

Business categories help Google understand what the business is.

I avoid selecting categories simply because they contain attractive keywords. The primary category should represent the business as accurately as possible, while secondary categories should only be added when they genuinely describe additional offerings.

The goal is relevance, not category stuffing.

## 3. Treat photos as part of the customer journey

For hospitality businesses, visual content matters.

Customers often want to understand the atmosphere, food, interior and overall experience before deciding whether to visit. I therefore see profile photos as more than decoration.

Useful photo types can include:

- Exterior and entrance
- Interior and seating
- Food or key products
- Team or service environment
- Current branded visuals

The images should help someone understand what to expect from the business.

## 4. Connect the profile to a useful website experience

A Google Business Profile can generate website visits, but sending users to a website is only useful if the landing experience answers their next question.

For a restaurant, that often means making the menu, location and contact actions easy to find.

On the restaurant project I worked on, the website's `/menu/` page recorded 2,216 organic views and 1,181 active users during the measured period, with an average engagement time of about 58 seconds.

That reinforced the importance of connecting local discovery with a useful on-site experience.

## 5. Measure customer actions, not only profile views

Profile views show visibility, but customer actions provide more context.

During the measured project period, the restaurant's Google Business Profile recorded:

- 17,122 profile views
- 4,170 direction requests
- 642 calls
- 286 website clicks

These are Google Business Profile metrics. I keep them separate from GA4 and Google Search Console data because each platform measures a different part of the journey.

I also avoid claiming that every action was caused by a specific profile optimization. Local performance can be influenced by brand awareness, location, demand, seasonality, reviews, marketing activity and other factors.

The data is most useful when it is treated as evidence of how customers interacted with the profile during the period.

## 6. Use Google Business Profile, Search Console and GA4 together

I use the three platforms for different questions.

### Google Business Profile

This helps me understand local profile visibility and direct actions such as calls, directions and website visits.

### Google Search Console

This helps me understand how the website appears in organic search, including queries, clicks, impressions and average position.

### Google Analytics 4

This helps me understand what visitors do after reaching the website, including sessions, engagement, landing pages and tracked events.

Looking at the platforms together creates a more complete picture without mixing their metrics.

For the same restaurant project, Google Search Console recorded 55.3K impressions and 2.12K clicks during the measured period, while GA4 recorded 2,804 organic sessions and 2,050 engaged sessions.

## 7. Review the profile as a customer

One of the simplest checks is to stop looking at the profile like a marketer.

I ask:

**If I discovered this business for the first time, could I quickly decide what it offers, where it is, when it is open and what I should do next?**

If the answer is unclear, there is usually something worth improving.

A technically complete profile can still provide a poor customer experience if important information is difficult to understand.

## My Google Business Profile checklist

For a local business, I would regularly review:

1. Business information and opening hours
2. Primary and secondary categories
3. Website and contact details
4. Map location and directions
5. Business description
6. Photos and visual freshness
7. Customer-facing information
8. Profile views and customer actions
9. Website landing experience
10. Search Console and GA4 performance alongside Google Business Profile data

## The key lesson

Google Business Profile optimization is not about making dozens of small changes just to look active.

The objective is to make the business easier to understand, discover and contact when someone is already searching locally.

For me, the strongest local SEO setup connects **Google Search + Google Maps + Google Business Profile + the website + analytics** into one measurable customer journey.
